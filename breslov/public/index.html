What this is

A daily piece of Rebbe Nachman, the day's Tehillim, the Tikkun HaKlali, the zmanim where you are, and a Torah for the week. It is meant to be opened once a day and read, not browsed.

Everything here is free, there is nothing to sign up for, and nothing about you is collected or sent anywhere. Your location, if you share it, is used to work out your zmanim and is not stored on the server.

How today's teaching is chosen

It is not random. The day's number picks a place in a fixed shuffled order of every lesson, so everyone opening the app on the same day sees the same teaching, and every lesson comes up once before any repeats. The Torah of the week works the same way but changes only on Sunday, and looks first for a lesson where Rebbe Nachman darshans a verse from that week's parsha.

The Tehillim follow the usual monthly division, by the day of the Hebrew month. In a 29-day month the 29th and 30th are said together.

Daily reminder

Subscribe in your phone's Calendar app and it will remind you every day, with the day's Tehillim on the reminder. It keeps working whether or not you open this site, and you can turn it off in Calendar whenever you like.

Sources

Every word of Torah in this app is fetched from Sefaria as you read it. Nothing is typed in by hand, nothing is paraphrased, and the edition and translator are shown under each passage. If a text cannot be reached, the app says so rather than showing something that might be wrong.

The texts

Sefaria — a free library of Jewish texts. Everything read in this app comes from there:

  • Tehillim

Some of these were written by Rebbe Nachman and some were recorded or compiled by his students, chiefly Reb Noson; where that is so it is noted beside the name. A sefer that Sefaria does not carry is left out of the app rather than guessed at.

Licences differ from one edition and translation to another. Each passage shows its own, underneath the text.

Zmanim

kosher-zmanim by BehindTheMath (LGPL-3.0), the JavaScript port of KosherJava by Eliyahu Hershfeld. The times are calculated on the server, not fetched.

Times are a guide. Follow your rav and your shul's luach.

The Jewish calendar

@hebcal/core by Michael J. Radwin (GPL-2.0) — the Hebrew date, the parsha, candle lighting, havdalah and the yomim tovim.

Everything else

Built with Node.js and Express, hosted on Render. No analytics, no trackers, no accounts.
