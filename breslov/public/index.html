What this is

A daily piece of Rebbe Nachman, the day's Tehillim, the Tikkun HaKlali, the zmanim where you are, and a Torah for the week. It is meant to be opened once a day and read, not browsed.

Everything here is free, there is nothing to sign up for, no account and no tracking of any kind. The place your zmanim are set to is sent to this server to work the times out, and is not stored anywhere. The privacy page says exactly what happens to it.

How today's teaching is chosen

It is not random. The day's number picks a place in a fixed shuffled order of every lesson, so everyone opening the app on the same day sees the same teaching, and every lesson comes up once before any repeats. The Torah of the week works the same way but changes only on Sunday. On a week with a yom tov in it, it looks for a lesson about the yom tov; otherwise for one where Rebbe Nachman or Reb Noson darshans a verse from that week's parsha. If it finds neither, the card says so rather than showing a lesson that has nothing to do with the week.

The Tehillim follow the usual monthly division, by the day of the Hebrew month. In a 29-day month the 29th and 30th are said together.

Daily reminder

Subscribe in your phone's Calendar app and it will remind you every day, with the day's Tehillim on the reminder. It keeps working whether or not you open this site, and you can turn it off in Calendar whenever you like.

Privacy, and the rules

No accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no trackers, and nothing sold. Your settings stay on this device. Nothing you read here is recorded against you, because there is no account for it to be recorded against.

You can also delete everything saved about you, here or on the privacy page — it takes one tap and nothing is kept back.

Sources

Every word of Torah in this app is fetched from Sefaria as you read it. Nothing is typed in by hand, nothing is paraphrased, and the edition and translator are shown under each passage. If a text cannot be reached, the app says so rather than showing something that might be wrong.

What stays on your device, and never leaves it

Your chosen place, minhag, theme, font, text size, reminder time, whether you want the English, the psalm you last reached in the Tikkun HaKlali, and a copy of the last day's learning so the app opens with no signal. All of it is kept in this browser's own storage. The only one that ever leaves this device is the place, which goes to this server to work the times out — the next section says exactly what happens to it. The button at the foot of this page clears every bit of it.

The one cookie

If the site has a password on it, getting past the password sets one cookie, bd_access, so you are not asked again on every page. It holds a fingerprint of the password, never the password itself. It cannot be read by any script, it is not sent to other sites, and it lasts a year unless you clear it.

That is the only cookie, and it exists solely to let you in — which is why there is no cookie banner here. A banner asks consent for cookies you did not need; there are none to ask about. Nothing is measured, remembered or shared.

What the server sees

To work out zmanim, the app sends a place — coordinates and a time zone — with each request. They are used to calculate the times and are then gone; there is nowhere to put them. If you tap "Use my current location", your phone asks your permission first, and the coordinates are rounded to about a kilometre before being sent — still far more precise than the times need, and nowhere near precise enough to point at an address.

Searches you type are sent to this server and passed on to Sefaria to be answered. The answer is held in the server's memory for a few hours so the same search is instant the second time — filed under the words you typed and nothing else, with no record of who asked, and gone whenever the server restarts. Nothing is written to disk, and there is no search history here or anywhere.

Like any website, the company hosting this one keeps ordinary server logs for a short period, and those include IP addresses. That is their doing, not this app's, and nothing here adds to it.

Who else is contacted

From your device: nobody but this server. No fonts, no scripts, no images, no analytics from anywhere else. You can check it yourself in any browser's network panel.

The server fetches the texts from Sefaria on your behalf, so Sefaria sees this server and never sees you. Zmanim and the Hebrew calendar are worked out on this server with no outside request at all.

The daily reminder

The reminder is a calendar subscription, not email. This site never sends email and has no mailing list, so there is nothing to unsubscribe from. Your Calendar app fetches the feed from this server now and then, which the server sees as an ordinary request; removing the subscription in Calendar stops it immediately.

Children

Nothing is collected from anyone of any age, so there is nothing to ask a parent's permission for. There is no sign-up, no age gate and no profile, for a child or for anybody else.

Delete everything

There is no account to close, because there is no account. What there is, you can remove right here: the settings saved in this browser, and the cookie that remembers you got past the password.

Changes

If any of this changes, it changes here. This page is part of the app rather than a separate document that can quietly go out of date, and the version it belongs to is shown at the foot of the About page.

The Torah is not ours

Every passage belongs to whoever published that edition or made that translation, and each one is named under the text it belongs to, with a link to it on Sefaria. Licences differ from one edition to the next. Nothing here is written, paraphrased or translated by this app; where a text cannot be fetched, the app says so instead of filling the gap.

Times are a guide

Zmanim are calculated, and calculations rest on the opinion chosen, the place given and a model of the horizon that no model gets exactly right. Follow your rav and your shul's luach. Do not rely on this app for anything that matters halachically, and do not rely on it where being wrong would be serious.
